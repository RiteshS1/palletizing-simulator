# Stacking rules

`checkPlacement()` answers one question for every algorithm: is this placement allowed? Its checks
run cheapest first and the first failure returns immediately (**fail fast**), because a packer calls
it thousands of times per pallet.

| Order | Check | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inside the pallet and under max height | O(1) |
| 2 | Total load under max | O(1) |
| 3 | No overlap; nothing above the spot; who supports us | O(n) single pass |
| 4 | Support ratio ≥ 75% | O(1) after the pass |
| 5 | Center inside the support polygon | O(k log k) |
| 6 | Not heavier than any supporter | O(supporters) |

## Support ratio

Supported area = the sum of footprint overlaps with cartons whose **top equals our bottom** (or the
whole base on the deck). Supporters cannot overlap each other (they would collide), so summing is safe.

## The support polygon

Something stays standing when its **center of mass, projected straight down, falls inside the outline
of its contacts**: the support polygon, which is the **convex hull** of the contact areas. A bridge
across two pillars has only 50% of its area supported but is perfectly stable, because its centre is
inside the hull. The live model shows a carton sliding across two pillars: blue is the hull, the
carton turns orange when its centre leaves it.

<Callout title="A neat fact">
Any line through a rectangle's centre splits it into two equal halves. So if more than 50% of the base
is supported, the centre is always inside the hull. With our 75% threshold the polygon rule never
fires; it matters once you relax the ratio below one half.
</Callout>

## Convex hull: Andrew's monotone chain

Sort the points by x then y; sweep left to right building the lower hull, then right to left for the
upper hull. Whenever the last three points don't turn left, pop the middle one. **O(k log k)**, the
sort dominating; each point is pushed and popped at most once per sweep.

The turn test is the 2D cross product:

```cpp
int64_t cross(P2 o, P2 a, P2 b) {
    return (a.x - o.x) * (b.y - o.y) - (a.y - o.y) * (b.x - o.x);  // > 0: left turn
}
```

Box centres can land on half millimetres, so the code doubles every coordinate and the maths stays in
exact integers.

## Load bearing and reachability

- **Load bearing:** a carton may not rest on a lighter one. Production systems sum the load down the
  whole "rests on" graph against each carton's crush strength.
- **Reachable from above:** a top-down gripper cannot slide a carton under an overhang, even into free
  space. Physically valid is not the same as robot-executable.
