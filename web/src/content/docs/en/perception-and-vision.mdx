# Perception and vision

Before a robot can place a carton, it has to **see** it: where it is, how big it is, and which way
it is turned. In mixed-case cells nothing is known in advance with millimetre accuracy, so 3D
vision runs on every pick.

## From camera to point cloud

Industrial 3D cameras measure depth for every pixel:

| Technology | How it measures | Good at |
|---|---|---|
| Structured light | projects a known pattern and triangulates its distortion | high accuracy on static scenes |
| Stereo | matches features between two cameras | outdoor light, larger volumes |
| Time-of-flight | times reflected light | speed, compact sensors |
| Laser line scanning | sweeps a laser line, like the live model | very precise profiles on conveyors |

Each depth pixel becomes a 3D point: the result is a **point cloud**, often a million points. The
live model sweeps a laser plane across the pallet and colours the points by height, exactly how
engineers look at depth data.

## Finding cartons in the cloud

1. **Remove what you know:** crop to the pallet volume, subtract the floor and the pallet itself.
2. **Find planes:** carton tops are flat rectangles. **RANSAC** (random sample consensus) picks three
   random points, fits a plane, counts how many points agree, and keeps the best plane after many
   tries. It ignores outliers by design.
3. **Segment:** split each plane into separate rectangles using edges and depth jumps; deep-learning
   segmentation helps when cartons are printed or tightly packed.
4. **Estimate the pose:** a rectangle's centre, size and yaw give the pick pose, in the camera frame.
5. **Transform:** chain the hand-eye calibration (`T_base_camera`) to get the pose in the robot base frame.

## Data structures that make it fast

- **k-d trees** and **voxel grids** answer "which points are near this one?" in roughly O(log n)
  instead of O(n), the backbone of normal estimation, clustering and downsampling.
- **Downsampling** a million points into 1 cm voxels keeps the shape and cuts the work by 10–100×.

## Hand-eye calibration

The camera and the robot must agree on where things are. Calibration moves the robot through
known poses while the camera watches a target, then solves for the fixed transform between them.
A 2 mm calibration error becomes a 2 mm placement error on every carton.
